Explain this figure

Streets are colored by an approximate street water depth — the chosen water surface minus the street's ground elevation (USGS 1-m LiDAR sampled ~25 m along each street; NAVD88, the same datum as every Barnacle landmark) — using the SAME depth bands as the 342 Bay flood map. It is an approximation: LiDAR carries a few tenths of a foot of error near buildings; one town-wide water surface is exactly right for tidal flooding (the bay is one connected level) but only a rough envelope for rain flooding, where each low spot ponds by its own drainage. Water on a property is a function of the building's base height relative to its street: a house that sits well above the street sees far less water than the street number suggests; one at street level sees all of it — the colors deliberately stop at the streets because this map does not know any building's floor height. The forecast slider replays the predicted bay level from the live forecast (tide pathway only — rain-burst overlays are calibrated to the 342 catchment and are not claimed town-wide). The "342 Bay Ave relative" toggle restates all heights in inches vs the SW grate (3.52 ft NAVD88), the reference every other Barnacle surface uses. Cross-reference: the 342 corner (●) takes tidal water at ~6.34 ft MLLW; each street's popup gives its own onset, so "floods before/after 342" is directly readable.

Elevations: USGS 3DEP 1-m LiDAR (NAVD88) · streets: OpenStreetMap contributors · conversions: NAVD88 = MLLW − 2.82 ft at Sandy Hook; SW grate = 3.52 ft NAVD88 = 6.34 ft MLLW.
