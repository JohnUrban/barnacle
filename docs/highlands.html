Explain this figure

Streets are colored by an approximate street water depth — the chosen water surface minus the street's ground elevation (USGS 1-m LiDAR sampled ~25 m along each street; NAVD88, the same datum as every Barnacle landmark) — using the SAME depth bands as the 342 Bay flood map. It is an approximation: LiDAR carries a few tenths of a foot of error near buildings; one town-wide water surface is exactly right for tidal flooding (the bay is one connected level) but only a rough envelope for rain. Water on a property is a function of the building's base height relative to its street: a house well above its street sees far less water than the street number suggests; one at street level sees all of it — colors deliberately stop at the streets because this map does not know any building's floor height. The rain view replays the 342-corner rain+tide water level (the calibrated tank hydrograph) instead of the bay tide. It is calibrated at the Bay/Central corner: expect it to be close across downtown Highlands with error growing with distance from that corner (still directionally right), and it is not applied to Atlantic Highlands, Leonardo, or Sea Bright — different hillsides, different drainage; those streets always show the tidal surface. During burst-capable hours (thunder/heavy-rain forecast) the rain view shows the burst potential — the level a convective cell could fill to at any point in those hours (navy band on the mini chart); a quiet forecast makes the rain view identical to the tide view, which is honesty, not a bug. The base map (water, parks, sand, Route 36) is built from OpenStreetMap data. Cross-reference: the 342 corner (●) takes tidal water at ~6.34 ft MLLW; each street's popup gives its own onset, so "floods before/after 342" is directly readable.
