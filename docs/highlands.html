Explain this figure

Streets are colored by an approximate street water depth: the chosen water-surface elevation minus the street's ground elevation (USGS 1-m LiDAR, sampled about every 25 m along the street network; NAVD88 datum, the same one every Barnacle landmark uses). It is an approximation/guess: LiDAR carries ~a few tenths of a foot of error near buildings, a single water surface across town is exactly right for tidal flooding (the bay is one connected level) but only a rough envelope for rain flooding, where each low spot ponds by its own drainage. Water on a property is a function of the building's base height relative to its street: a house or shop that sits well above the street sees far less water than the street number suggests; one at street level sees all of it. The colors deliberately stop at the streets — this map does not claim to know any building's floor height. Cross-reference: the 342 Bay corner (●) starts taking tidal water at about 6.34 ft MLLW; each street's popup gives the same onset number for that street, so "floods before/after the 342 corner" is directly readable. Rain events at the 342 corner are modeled in detail on the main page; no such calibration exists yet elsewhere in town.

Elevations: USGS 3DEP 1-m LiDAR (NAVD88) · streets: OpenStreetMap contributors · conversions: NAVD88 = MLLW − 2.82 ft at Sandy Hook.
