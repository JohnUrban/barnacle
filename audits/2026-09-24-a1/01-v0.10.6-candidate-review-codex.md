## Findings requiring a reply and repair

### R1 — HIGH: the outage table does not use the curve's fallback estimator

`forecast/outlook.py:179-190` only accepts `guidance_decay` from `est`.
It ignores `observed_decay` and `typical_offset`, then uses `persisted_surge`
(which is None when the gauge is stale/unavailable) or astronomy alone.
Consequently the claimed ONE estimator is not used across the full ladder.

Frozen fixture, next future tide 2026-09-23 18:19-04:00, no hourly NWPS/P-ETSS
or advisory row:

| Available information | Table ft MLLW | Hourly estimator at that same tide | Difference |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| No usable reading, mean +0.54 ft | 5.11 (`astro`) | 5.65 (`typical_offset`) | +0.54 ft |
| Reading +2.5 ft from 10 h earlier | 5.11 (`astro`) | 6.734 (`observed_decay`) | +1.624 ft |

These are synthetic outage probes on committed fixtures, not observed floods.
Table/card tide values can therefore understate what the curve/maps predict.
With a fresh reading 50 min old, the table also restarts decay at issuance
rather than the reading time (7.08 vs 7.049 ft at the fixture tide).

**Required:** use the selected estimator/anchor for every fallback rung,
including age and typical offset; preserve explicit advisory semantics.
Update source labels, ledger admission and tests as needed. The current test
named `test_line_and_table_agree_at_every_tide` only checks `guidance_decay`;
extend it to no guidance + fresh/stale-download/stale-state/no reading and
source gaps. Check the table, cards and map payload, not only `est()` itself.

### R2 — MEDIUM: assumed guidance is still depicted as NWS/P-ETSS guidance

`forecast/outlook_page.py:411` gives triangles only to `persist_decay`.
New `guidance_decay` gets a circle, while the displayed legend says circle =
NWS/P-ETSS guidance, triangle = decayed persistence. Browser inspection confirms
Monday-Wednesday's assumed tail appears as circles. The intro at lines 105-107
still says the tail uses "this hour's surge", unlike the actual last-guidance
anchor. This defeats the source distinction that motivated checklist item 1.

The spec's Tide-keyed pathway (`model/v0.10.6.md:331-339`) still prescribes
constant observed surge / zero-surge `astronomical-only-degraded`, contradicting
the new formula and typical-offset fallback documented earlier in the spec.

**Required:** propagate new source meanings through both charts, legends,
intro and spec; label assumed guidance as assumed. Preserve the successful
upper-chart bands. Verify both charts visually after regeneration. Also put
advisory markers at their actual times or explicitly identify the snapping:
current hour-key placement can shift a seven-day marker up to 59 minutes early
(and a half-hour landing marker can shift either way).

### R3 — MEDIUM: skill scoring silently combines different model formulas

The spec's surfaces table promises `outlook_log` is scored by version.
`forecast/outlook.py:878` (`score_shadow`) does not examine `model_version`,
and `build_outlook_7d_field` passes the entire historical ledger. v0.10.5's
48-h-to-zero and v0.10.6's 36-h-to-mean values consequently share one
`persist_decay` score and readiness verdict; production/outlook formulas also
change across the bump.

Synthetic proof: 28 good v0.10.5 tides plus one bad v0.10.6 issuance returns
`READY`, n=28, candidate MAE 0.071 vs baseline 1.0. The new formula actually
has one scored tide and an error of 4 ft in that example. This is not a claim
about live readiness (the live sample is still small); it demonstrates why
old evidence cannot qualify the new formula.

**Required:** score versioned methods separately or explicitly select the
reviewed version; label the cohort and retain all historical rows. Test mixed
versions, including the readiness count. Do not fix this by rewriting ledgers.

### R4 — MEDIUM: a malformed mean timestamp aborts the production build

`forecast/surge_decay.py:133-139` accepts a parseable naive timestamp, then
subtracts it from aware UTC outside the guarded block. A JSON record with
`mean_ft=.54`, `n_hours=8000`, `computed_utc="2026-09-23T00:00:00"` raises
`TypeError: can't subtract offset-naive and offset-aware datetimes` through
`build_forecast()`. No fallback forecast is produced, despite the new input's
stated malformed-record fallback policy.

**Required:** validate aware timestamps and the record before date arithmetic;
malformed input must use the documented degraded constant. Exercise this via
the whole build, not just a happy-path resolve_mean test. Check mean warm-write
admission too so non-finite predictions cannot replace a good record.

### R5 — MEDIUM: stale-state provenance describes the wrong reading

`forecast/flood_forecast_daily.py:2555-2556` fills top-level
`water_series_input.observation_time/age_min` from the attempted live fetch,
not the selected anchor. The nested `decay` object and detail use the selected
state correctly. A fixture with a 10-hour state reading yields top-level
`age_min=90`, but `decay.age_h=10`; real empty downloads leave the top-level
stamp/age empty even when the saved reading is used.

The spec also declares input health for the new state input, but there is no
`surge_state` entry; failed state writes only print a warning.

**Required:** derive forcing provenance from the selected anchor, retain
live-fetch diagnostics separately, and expose state admission/write failures
(or explicitly document the health entry carrying them). Test consumers and
metadata in every outage rung. The point of this ladder is usable, honestly
aged information during an outage.

### R6 — MEDIUM / evidence gap: item 5's blanket deferral is too broad

The cited `surge_decay_views.py` scores all hours, high tides, the plug band
and storm starts; it has no separate low-tide mask. The spec's item 5 marks
low-tide evidence DONE anyway. Neither this surge-only score nor unchanged
tank arithmetic is a comparison of candidate rain-driven flood depth.

There is no complete raw issuance-input archive here, but there ARE saved
issuance-time rain windows. Primary example:
`3a6c96faf7ebbf98c3e69322948aa5c1ddbbcedc:docs/forecast.json`, generated
2026-09-13T03:14:36Z, before the Sep13 floods. Its 09:58 tide's
`rain_window_3h` preserves hourly QPF from 07:00-12:00 station time (0.1833 in/h
at 07:00, then 0.09167 in/h); it also preserves the issued tide/water curves.
The [event record](../../assets/observations/2026-09-13/README.md) identifies
this exact commit and measured outcomes. These partial windows are not enough
to claim an unrestricted perfect replay; initial storage and uncovered hours
must remain explicit uncertainties. They are enough to investigate a bounded
comparison rather than declare every issuance-time comparison impossible.

A simple mechanism probe further shows why the plug-band surge metric is not
a substitute: with the SAME synthetic 0.3 in/h rain for 3 h, raising bay level
from 3.1 to 3.5 NAVD88 raises predicted tank water from 4.144610 to 4.320703
NAVD88 (~2.11 inches). This is sensitivity, NOT skill evidence. Also the live
comparison report's identical tank result does not prove insensitivity: its
curve reaches above 4 ft NAVD88, contradicting the spec's explanation that the
bay stayed below the relevant band. Its dry near-term rain cannot test a wet
compound event.

**Required before a broad waiver:** add the missing low-tide score; assess
usable archived event windows, run controlled wet scenarios spanning below,
within and above the drain band, and compare tank levels, threshold durations
and map depths under identical inputs. Separate reconstructed/observed-rain
hindcasts from as-issued forecast skill. Identify the exact residual that
really needs prospective collection; then request a narrower owner deferral.

## Recommendations on John's two decisions

**Item 2:** a temporary, explicitly experimental retention of advisory
corrections is reasonable, subject to the repairs above and prospective
logging of raw hourly NWPS, advisory rows, astronomy and corrected output,
with issuance/retrieval/target timestamps and model version. Compare raw and
corrected errors at high, mid and low tide against later gauge observations.
The 0-0.4 ft range is ONE capture's range, not a guaranteed bound: the
candidate's later 04:10:05Z artifact has six zero corrections. Moreover
0.4 ft is 4.8 inches, which can matter near a street/drain threshold. Do not
call it negligible or validated. John's "sounds sensible" is support for this
approach; it is not recorded here as a promotion decision.

**Item 5:** Claude's proposal is to accept missing historical end-to-end
rain-event validation, using plug-band surge evidence as partial support.
It is not yet an implemented archive. Recommend BOTH a bounded comparison
using what exists (R6) and a prospective replay-input archive: rain forecasts,
astro/gauge/surge inputs, source issuance times, selected fallback state/mean,
model version, and relevant tank initialization. Capture unavailable inputs
as unavailable. Accept only the remaining multi-event skill limitation after
those checks; do not hold a bounded release indefinitely waiting for future
storms, and do not claim the validation has already happened.

No final promotion decision is needed from John before Claude repairs these
findings. Scope approval and scientific deferrals do not waive coding defects.
